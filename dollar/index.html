Argentina in Numbers

The dollar

Argentina has had many dollars at once: the official rate, the wholesale rate banks trade at, the informal "blue" on the street, and the MEP and CCL rates implied by buying a bond in pesos and selling it in dollars. The gap between them measures how tight exchange controls were.

The gap

How far the blue and CCL rates stood above the official one, monthly average. The gap opens when the government restricts buying dollars (the "cepo") and closes when restrictions are lifted.

Is the dollar cheap or expensive?

A rough guide, not a real exchange rate: it adjusts only for Argentine inflation, not for inflation in the United States or trading partners (a few percent a year), so long-run comparisons overstate how expensive the past dollar was. 2007–2015 is shaded because the official CPI understated inflation then, which makes those years look cheaper than they were.
